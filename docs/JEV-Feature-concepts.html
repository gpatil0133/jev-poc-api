JEV Feature Concepts
Sogolytics platform · new features built on JEV

New features JEV makes possible

JEV only makes decisions: given one short text and a list of options, it returns a probability for each option. It does not store, count, join or trend anything. Every feature on this page is therefore split into the JEV decision and the platform work around it, so it is clear which part is AI and which part is ordinary product engineering.

Everything on this page is a proposal, not current product behaviour. Examples are illustrative, not measured. Each feature was checked against the AI Report's open-ended analysis (theme impact, dissonance, cohorts) so it does not repeat what that already does.

Part 1

Built on decisions

JEV decides on one item at a time. The platform saves each decision and does the counting, linking and reporting, the same way it already handles closed-question answers.

Virtual Questions

Lead featureMedium effort
Defined in Design (or at account level) · answers appear in Data, Reports, Filters, Logic, Rules & Alerts and Export

In simple words: a closed survey question nobody had to answer, because JEV answers it from what each respondent already wrote. The answer then behaves like a real question everywhere in the product.

What we have today
  • Closed questions only exist if respondents were asked them.
  • The AI Report's open-ended analysis finds themes itself and shows them as report panels.
  • A question you think of after the survey closes needs a new survey, or someone reading every comment.
What the user gets
  • Ask after: add a question to a closed survey and get an answer per respondent.
  • Say it once: skip rating questions a respondent already covered in their comment.
  • Question library: save questions at account level and use them as Logic or Rules & Alerts conditions.
The JEV decision
Input
One respondent's comment, plus the question the admin wrote
Options
The admin's answer options, plus "not mentioned"
Output
A probability per option, for example "Yes, negative 0.95"
How often
One decision per respondent per Virtual Question. For "ask after" there are two ways to keep it light: decide on a random sample of about 400 comments, which gives the share to within about ±5 points at 95% confidence whatever the survey size; or apply it only to new responses going forward. The full back-run is an optional background job.

Admin writes the question and optionsPlatformSaved like a question definition

Answer a respondentJEVOne decision on that respondent's comment
Save the answer and confidencePlatformStored as an inferred response value, apart from real answers
Use in Omni, Cross-Tab, Filters, Logic, Alerts, ExportPlatformRead like any closed answer, with an "inferred" marker

Why it's new

Today you can only analyse what you remembered to ask. This lets you ask afterwards and get an answer per respondent the rest of the product can use. The AI Report discovers themes; Virtual Questions answers the customer's own question.

Why it needs JEV

The options are defined per request, so nothing is retrained. Each answer is a cheap decision with a calibrated probability, fast enough for the live "say it once" case.

Guardrails: inferred answers are marked in reports and flagged in exports. Low confidence lands on "not mentioned". In live surveys a question is never skipped on low confidence. Virtual Questions cannot target personal details such as names or health conditions in anonymous projects.

Survey Coach

Low effortLow risk
Design → question builder (before launch) · Design → survey overview (after launch)

In simple words: before launch, flag badly worded questions. After launch, point out what respondents keep writing about that no question asks.

What we have today

Insights describe respondents, not the survey. To confirm: whether the builder checks question wording at all today.

What the user gets

Spell-checker style warnings with a suggested rewrite, and an occasional "add a question about X?" prompt after launch.

The JEV decisions
Before launch
Input: one question and its answer options. Options: leading, double-barrelled, vague time frame, jargon, sensitive, overlapping or missing answer options, fine. One decision per question saved.
After launch
Input: one comment. Options: the survey's own questions, plus "none of these". One decision per comment, or per sampled comment.

Fix Tracker

Medium effortNeeds repeat respondents
CX and EX programs · contacts linked through Directories

In simple words: when a customer complains and later answers another survey, check whether their earlier problem is now fixed.

What we have today

The AI Report analyses one project at one point in time. Nothing follows one person's issue from one survey to the next.

What the user gets

A fix rate per issue, branch and team, and the time it takes to fix.

The JEV decisions
Decision 1
When a complaint arrives. Input: the comment. Options: the account's issue list (late delivery, billing error…). Output: "late delivery 0.93".
Decision 2
When the same contact answers again. Input: the new comment, with the stored issue named in the question. Options: fixed, still happening, worse, not mentioned.
How often
At most two decisions per complaint, and only for contacts who come back. Very low volume.

Feedback Owners

Low–medium effortPermissions
Account settings → teams · weekly inbox per team lead

In simple words: route every comment to the team that owns it, so each department head gets their own feedback without opening the full report.

What we have today

Reports are organised by question and theme. Someone has to read them and decide who should act. Report Viewers and Data Visibility Rules control who sees what, but not by which team owns the issue.

What the user gets

A weekly inbox per team lead with only the feedback that team owns.

The JEV decision
Input
One comment
Options
The account's own team list, each with a one-line description (Billing, IT, Facilities, Front desk…). Large organisations pick the department first, then the team.
How often
One decision per comment, when the response arrives
Who does what
StepWhoWhat happens

Build each team's inboxPlatformFilter saved assignments, respecting Data Visibility Rules and the four user types

Example
Facilities: 46 comments this week, 38 negative. Most are about car park lighting and the broken lift at Riverside.

Watch-outs: a team lead must only see what they are allowed to (Admin User, Sub-User, Sub-User as Admin, Report Viewer, plus Data Visibility Rules).

Part 2

Decision only

Each of these is one JEV decision that triggers one action straight away. No totals, trends or history are needed.

Offer a callback when it's needed

Low effortLive page
Live survey page · after a Text Box answer

In simple words: ask "Would you like someone to contact you?" only of people describing an unresolved problem.

What we have today

Follow-up questions are triggered by the score, so a contact-me question goes to everyone with a low score, or to everyone.

What the user gets

Service recovery in the moment, offered only where it is needed.

The JEV decision
Input
The comment
Options
Describes an unresolved problem / does not
How often
One decision when the respondent clicks Next
Who does what
StepWhoWhat happens
DecideJEVUnresolved problem or not
Show the contact questionPlatformExisting Question Display Logic, with the decision as its condition
Example

"My refund still hasn't arrived after three weeks" → unresolved 0.94 → the contact question appears. "Quick and easy, thanks" → the normal next page.

Watch-outs: runs in front of the respondent, so it needs a strict time limit and falls back to the normal path silently.

Hold the invitation at a bad moment

Low effortLow risk
Distribute → automated invitations · Touch Rules · Activities

In simple words: don't send a survey to someone whose latest Activity says it is a bad time to ask.

What we have today

Touch Rules decide who gets invited using structured values. Activity Fields drive Touch Rules only as structured values, so a free-text note like "bereavement" is ignored.

What the user gets

Fewer badly timed invitations, and fewer complaints about them.

The JEV decision
Input
The latest Activity note for the contact
Options
Send / hold / don't send
How often
One decision per invitation that has a recent Activity note
Who does what

ApplyPlatformThe decision is one condition inside Touch Rules. The rules themselves stay deterministic.

Turn free text into a category at submit

Top pickLow effort
Live survey page · free-text fields such as job title or industry

In simple words: when someone types a job title or industry, store the matching category too, so quotas, logic and segments can use it immediately.

What we have today

Quotas, logic and segments work on closed answers. A typed answer like "Sr. Eng. Manager" can't count toward a "Senior management" quota until someone recodes it by hand.

What the user gets

Respondents type naturally; the data arrives already categorised.

The JEV decision
Input
The typed answer
Options
The category list the author attached to that question (seniority, industry, department…), plus "can't tell"

"This might identify you" warning

Low effortLive page
Live survey page · anonymous and semi-anonymous EX projects

In simple words: before an employee submits, warn them if their comment could reveal who they are, so they can edit it.

What we have today

Anonymity removes email, IP and participation time. Nothing checks whether the words themselves give the writer away.

What the user gets

Employees trust anonymous surveys more, and fewer identifying comments reach managers.

The JEV decision
Input
The comment
Options
Identifies the writer / names someone else / safe
How often
One decision per comment, at submit
Who does what
StepWhoWhat happens
DecideJEVIdentifying or safe

Example

"As the only night-shift nurse in Ward 4, I never get breaks" → identifies the writer 0.93 → the note appears.

Watch-outs: never blocks and never stores anything extra. Only the comment text is sent. This is the respondent-side counterpart of the EX anonymity flag on the JEV Scope page.

Suggested order

1

Free text to category. One decision, almost no platform work, and it unblocks quotas and segmentation.

2

Survey Coach, before launch. One decision per question saved, low risk, seen by every author.

3

Callback offer and invitation hold. Single decisions that trigger existing logic and Touch Rules.

4

Virtual Questions, going forward plus sampled estimates for past data.

5

Fix Tracker and Feedback Owners, once contact linkage and team permissions are designed.

Open questions

JEV
  • Pricing and rate limits per decision, which decide whether full back-runs or only samples are affordable.
  • Response time, for the three features that run on the live survey page.
  • Data residency and HIPAA handling.
Platform
  • Can contacts be linked across surveys today, for Fix Tracker?
  • Do replies to invitation emails reach Sogolytics?
  • Does the builder check question wording today?
Product
  • Should inferred answers count in headline metrics, or only in breakdowns?
  • Do authors accept that some respondents see fewer questions with "say it once"?
